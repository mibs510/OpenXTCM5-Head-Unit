# OpenXTCM5 Power Control Rationale

**Status:** Working design record  
**Last updated:** 2026-08-23  
**Scope:** Why each controlled rail or control signal exists, how it is expected to behave, and when it should _not_ be used.

This document records the intent behind the power architecture while the schematic is still changing. It is deliberately more explicit than the schematic: a net name says *what is connected*; this document says *why it may be turned off*, *who is allowed to turn it off*, and *what must happen before it is turned back on*.

It is not a substitute for validating component limits, the final display power-up requirements, measured crank behavior, or a safety review of vehicle-facing functions.

## 1. Core Decision

OpenXTCM5 has two different kinds of power control:

1. **Global power control** decides whether the head unit is awake at all.
2. **Branch power control** isolates an external or recoverable subsystem while the main computer stays alive.

These must not be conflated. A rail does not need a branch switch merely because it is generated from `5V_SYS_GOOD`. `5V_SYS_GOOD` is the global "the main 5 V rail is valid" condition. It starts the regular 3.3 V and 1.8 V regulators. Branch switches exist only where independent isolation, external-short protection, a controlled restart, or load shedding is useful.

The first-board priority is simple:

- Keep the protected input path, always-on supervisor, RP2354, and CM5 alive whenever the main 5 V buck-boost can sustain them.
- Remove the highest and least essential loads first during low input voltage or crank.
- Start every nonessential branch in the off state until RP2354 firmware has made an intentional decision.
- Do not introduce a switch just because it feels tidy. Every switch adds BOM, voltage drop, inrush behavior, firmware states, and a potential failure mode.

## 2. Power Domains

| Domain | Purpose | Normal vehicle-off state | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Protected `+12V` | Input after fuse, TVS, and reverse-polarity protection | Present when BATT+ is present | Input protection hardware |
| `+3V3_AON` | Low-IQ always-on supervisor and ACC sensing | On | TPS7B8433-Q1 class regulator |
| `+5V_SYS` | Main CM5/system rail | Off | TPS552892-Q1 plus ACC/SYS_HOLD logic |
| `5V_SYS_GOOD` | Valid-main-rail indication, not a power source | Low | TPS552892-Q1 power-good output |
| `+3V3` | Main digital rail | Off with `+5V_SYS` | 3.3 V regulator enabled by `5V_SYS_GOOD` |
| `+1V8` | Main 1.8 V rail | Off with `+5V_SYS` | 1.8 V regulator enabled by `5V_SYS_GOOD` |

`+3V3_AON` is intentionally separate from `+3V3`. It exists so ACC sensing and orderly global power control remain possible while the CM5/RP2354 system is off.

## 3. When a Branch Needs a Switch

Add a branch switch only when at least one of these statements is true:

1. **It reaches an external connector or harness.** A fault there should not collapse the shared rail. USB VBUS and camera power are the clearest examples.
2. **It is a material load that can be shed without losing the core computer.** Backlight, amplifier, USB VBUS, and camera are the primary crank-shedding candidates.
3. **The device needs a real power cycle to recover.** Some display, touch, and USB/video devices cannot always be recovered by reset alone.
4. **The device can back-power another domain while it is off.** A switch or deliberately selected I/O isolation scheme may be necessary.
5. **Software needs a meaningful user-visible policy.** For example: disable USB charging while asleep, enable a reverse camera only when needed, or restart a stuck touch controller.

Do **not** add a switch merely for these reasons:

- The rail is generated by `5V_SYS_GOOD` already.
- The load is a tiny always-on digital load with no independent reset or protection benefit.
- The goal is to compensate for an undefined power-up sequence. Fix the sequence instead.
- The branch would be turned off during crank but requires a lengthy re-enumeration/re-initialization that makes the user experience worse than its load reduction is worth.

## 4. Global State Machine

### Vehicle off / standby

- BATT+ is still present at the rear connector.
- The protected input path and `+3V3_AON` remain alive.
- The main TPS552892-Q1 is disabled, so `+5V_SYS`, CM5, RP2354, display, touch, audio, USB VBUS, camera, and fan are off.
- ACC monitoring remains available through the always-on domain.

### ACC on / boot

1. The always-on logic recognizes `ACC_OK`.
2. The global enable path releases the TPS552892-Q1.
3. `+5V_SYS` stabilizes and asserts `5V_SYS_GOOD`.
4. The main `+3V3` and `+1V8` regulators start.
5. RP2354 boots with all discretionary branch-enable GPIOs at their safe defaults.
6. Firmware brings up subsystems in an intentional order: display logic and bias, touch, USB VBUS, camera, backlight, then amplifier output.

### ACC off / controlled shutdown

1. `ACC_OK` falls. RP2354 starts a bounded shutdown lease by asserting `SYS_HOLD`; the diode-OR latch keeps `+5V_SYS` alive even though ACC is no longer valid.
2. RP2354 immediately disables high-load or externally exposed branches: amplifier output, backlight, LCD bias, touch, USB VBUS, and camera.
3. RP2354 sends `IGNITION_OFF_PENDING` to the CM5 over the normal CM5-to-RP2354 USB management link. A separate active-low attention interrupt can prompt the CM5 to read that message, but USB acknowledgements are authoritative.
4. CM5 returns `SHUTDOWN_ACCEPTED`, performs its orderly Android/Linux shutdown, then sends `SHUTDOWN_READY` immediately before its final power-off action.
5. RP2354 releases `SYS_HOLD` after `SHUTDOWN_READY` and a short guard interval, or releases it at the configured deadline if the CM5 never responds. The first-board value is 30 seconds, subject to measurement and product-policy review.

### `SYS_HOLD` is not hibernate

`SYS_HOLD` deliberately belongs to RP2354, not the CM5. The RP2354 sees the actual vehicle state, owns the battery-protection policy, and can release the latch after a CM5 or USB failure. A direct CM5 output could be high impedance during boot, could disappear during a crash, and cannot be allowed to keep the main rail alive indefinitely.

The first board treats ACC-off as a clean shutdown followed by a later cold boot. Releasing `SYS_HOLD` removes `+5V_SYS`; it does not preserve CM5 RAM. Do not describe this as hibernate. The CM5 does offer a configurable VPU-sleep shutdown mode, but it requires a separate parked-current and Android-resume validation effort before it can become a product policy. [Raspberry Pi configuration documentation](https://www.raspberrypi.com/documentation/computers/configuration.html)

The planned hardware implementation adds two deliberately separate paths:

- `RP2354_TO_CM5_ATTN_N`: active-low open-drain attention input on CM5 GPIO17 / module pin 50. It is only a doorbell for the USB management protocol.
- `CM5_PWR_BUT_ASSERT`: RP2354-controlled open-drain pull-down in parallel with SW1401, used only as a wake/shutdown fallback. It does not replace the USB protocol and must never be tied to `SYS_HOLD`.

`PMIC_ENABLE` remains outside normal RP2354 firmware control. The CM5 data sheet recommends only pulling it low after operating-system shutdown.

### Cold crank / low-input event

The buck-boost and protected input stage are responsible for preserving `+5V_SYS`; the branch switches do not create crank immunity by themselves. Firmware should shed loads in a predictable order if the input or main-rail monitor says that keeping the CM5 alive is at risk:

1. Force amplifier mute/standby.
2. Disable the backlight.
3. Disable USB VBUS.
4. Disable camera rails.
5. Optionally disable LCD bias if the remaining margin is still inadequate.

Normally keep the small digital rails (`+3V3`, `+1V8`, RP2354, and LCD logic) alive. Rebooting the display logic during every marginal crank may be more disruptive than the small current saving is worthwhile.

## 5. Branch-by-Branch Decision Record

### 5.1 `LCD_1V8_SW` - retained LCD logic recovery switch

| Item | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Upstream rail | `+1V8` |
| Selected part | AP22913W6-7 |
| Firmware control | `LCD_1V8_EN` from RP2354 |
| Downstream loads | LCD pins 2/3 (`TFT1V8`) and the B-side supply of the SN74LVC2T45 |
| Normal running state | On |
| Vehicle off | Off because `+1V8` is globally off |
| Crank policy | Normally remain on |
| Primary reason | Independent LCD logic isolation and a true panel-logic power-cycle recovery path |

This switch was **not** added primarily for cold-crank shedding. The LCD's 1.8 V logic draw is modest. Its value is that firmware can hold the panel logic off while the rest of the system remains alive, or force it through a real recovery cycle after a failed display initialization.

**Current first-board decision: retain this switch.** It gives firmware the ability to sequence the LCD logic deliberately at startup and to force a true LCD-logic reset after a cold crank or failed panel initialization. If later field testing shows that reset plus bias control are always sufficient, it can be reconsidered as a BOM-reduction option; it is not being removed from the current design.

The AP22913 is used here because it supports a 1.8 V input and provides controlled switching with reverse-current blocking. The AP22653 is not appropriate for this branch because its input-voltage range starts at 3.0 V.

### 5.2 LCD level shifter A-side `+3V3` - intentionally unswitched

| Item | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Device | SN74LVC2T45 |
| A-side supply | Main `+3V3` |
| B-side supply | `LCD_1V8_SW` |
| Direction | A to B: CM5/RP-side controls toward the LCD |
| A-side control signals | `LCD_RESET`, `LCD_STBYB` |
| Default state | 100 k pulldowns keep source and panel control signals low |

Do not add a second switch to VCCA merely because VCCB is switched. VCCA belongs to the controller domain and consumes very little current. With VCCB absent, the translator's power-off isolation keeps the B-side high impedance, and the B-side pulldowns hold LCD reset/standby at a safe level. Switching VCCA adds another rail transition with almost no load-shedding benefit.

### 5.3 `LCD_5V_BIAS_SW` - retain

| Item | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Upstream rail | `+5V_SYS` |
| Selected part | AP22653W6-7 |
| Firmware control | `LCD_BIAS_EN` from RP2354 |
| Downstream load | TPS65150 and LCD bias rails: AVDD, VGH, VGL, VCOM |
| Normal running state | On after LCD logic is stable |
| Vehicle off | Off |
| Crank policy | Optional late-stage shed; off before hard display recovery |
| Primary reason | Independent control of a high-energy display bias domain and clean panel restart |

The TPS65150 output rails are not ordinary logic rails. They include the positive and negative LCD analog/bias voltages. Being able to remove its 5 V input lets firmware stop the panel bias cleanly, eliminate a display-related fault from the main rail, and perform a known reinitialization sequence.

Suggested sequencing on bring-up is: panel logic 1.8 V -> LCD bias switch -> wait for bias rails to settle -> keep reset/standby asserted until the display interface is configured -> release per the panel's verified requirements. The exact delays remain a bench-validation item.

### 5.4 `TOUCH_3V3_SW` - retained touch recovery switch

| Item | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Upstream rail | `+3V3` |
| Selected part | AP22653W6-7 |
| Firmware control | `TOUCH_3V3_EN` from RP2354 |
| Downstream load | GT911 touch controller and its local pullups/logic |
| Normal running state | On after main 3.3 V is stable |
| Vehicle off | Off |
| Crank policy | Usually leave on; it is not a major load |
| Primary reason | Recover a stuck touch controller without rebooting CM5/RP2354; isolate the external touch flex domain |

**Current first-board decision: retain this switch.** Firmware will have a real recovery action: power off, place host I2C/reset/interrupt lines in safe states, delay for discharge, reapply 3.3 V, and reinitialize the GT911. This also gives the startup sequence one clear owner rather than relying on uncontrolled rail timing.

**Mandatory firmware rule:** before disabling `TOUCH_3V3_SW`, drive `TOUCH_RST` low or configure it as a high-impedance input. The 1 kOhm series resistor limits accidental injection current, but is not a substitute for placing the GPIO in a safe state while the GT911 is unpowered. Configure the host `TOUCH_INT` input with a pulldown, or otherwise mask its interrupt, while the touch rail is off.

Before finalizing it, verify that I2C, reset, and interrupt nets cannot parasitically power the GT911 while `TOUCH_3V3_SW` is off.

### 5.5 `BACKLIGHT_EN` - primary display load-shedding control

| Item | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Controlled device | TPS61165 backlight boost driver |
| Firmware control | `BACKLIGHT_EN` / driver CTRL path |
| Downstream load | Separate VLED+/VLED- backlight connector |
| Normal running state | Enabled only after valid panel bias and display initialization |
| Vehicle off | Off |
| Crank policy | First display-related load to disable |
| Primary reason | Fast, low-risk reduction of a meaningful display load without disturbing LCD digital state |

Backlight control is more valuable during crank than cutting `LCD_1V8_SW`. The display can continue to retain state or be reinitialized later while the LED boost load is removed immediately. A separate upstream 12 V load switch is optional and should only be added if the TPS61165 disable behavior, leakage, or fault containment proves inadequate.

### 5.6 `USB_VBUS_SW` - required external-port protection

| Item | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Upstream rail | `+5V_SYS` |
| Selected part | AP22653W6-7 |
| Firmware control | `USB_VBUS_EN` from RP2354 |
| Downstream load | User-accessible USB-A VBUS |
| Normal running state | On only after the USB host is ready |
| Vehicle off | Off |
| Crank policy | Off early |
| Primary reason | Current-limited protection for an external connector, plus software control of charging power |

This is a required branch switch. A user-accessible USB port can be shorted, can connect an unknown load, and can otherwise consume enough current to make a marginal crank event worse. Its current limit must be selected from the intended USB-port policy, not copied casually from another branch. Record the final current-limit resistor once the desired supported charging current and connector/harness limits are chosen.

Do not place the CM5's own 5 V input behind this switch.

### 5.6.1 `J1402` USB-C - self-powered CM5 maintenance port

| Item | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Connector role | USB 2.0 device/UFP port for CM5 maintenance, not a board power input or USB host port |
| Primary use | Assert `nRPIBOOT` and expose the selected non-Lite CM5's onboard eMMC/flash to a directly connected host for recovery or image flashing |
| Board power | Vehicle/bench input through the normal protected-input and `+5V_SYS` path |
| J1402 VBUS | NC; it does not power, back-feed, or connect to `+5V_SYS` |
| CC implementation | Independent 5.1 kOhm Rd pull-downs, R1409 and R1410, from CC1 and CC2 to GND. CM5 `CC1`/`CC2` power-supply negotiation pins are NC. |
| Relation to `USB_VBUS_SW` | None. `USB_VBUS_SW` supplies the user-facing USB-A host port; J1402 must not be connected to that branch. |

J1402 is intentionally a **self-powered data/maintenance port**. The head unit must already be powered from a suitably rated vehicle or bench supply before a host computer is connected. The host's VBUS is neither a startup source nor a system-current source, so the design never depends on a PC or powered hub to operate the CM5, display, storage, or other board loads.

The schematic leaves J1402 VBUS NC, retains the USB 2.0 ESD protection and CM5 data pair, and implements independent UFP Rd pull-downs on CC1 and CC2. Add only a protected, high-impedance VBUS-detect path if the final USB device implementation proves that host-attach detection is needed. Do not add a load switch or ideal-diode power path from J1402 VBUS to a board rail.

This role is distinct from the CM5-to-RP2354 USB host recovery link. J1402 lets an external computer recover the CM5's onboard storage; the internal CM5 host link lets the CM5 place the RP2354 in BOOTSEL and program its firmware.

References: [Raspberry Pi CM5 storage-flashing procedure](https://www.raspberrypi.com/documentation/computers/compute-module.html#flash-an-image-to-a-compute-module) and [CM5 datasheet, USB/CC pin definitions](https://datasheets.raspberrypi.com/cm5/cm5-datasheet.pdf).

### 5.7 `CAMERA_12V_SW` - required external-camera supply control

| Item | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Upstream rail | Protected `+12V` |
| Selected part | TPS1HC120CQDYCRQ1 high-side switch |
| Firmware control | `CAMERA_12V_EN` from RP2354 |
| Downstream load | Rear-camera connector power |
| Normal running state | Policy-dependent; normally off until a camera session is needed |
| Vehicle off | Off |
| Crank policy | Off early |
| Primary reason | Protect and control an external vehicle-facing power branch; report a branch fault |

The camera harness is external, so it deserves more robust treatment than a small local logic rail. The high-side switch provides controlled delivery and fault handling without placing an unfused, permanently live 12 V rail at the rear-camera connector.

The present starting point is a modest current limit, not a 1.5 A commitment. Confirm the actual camera's startup current, cable length, and connector rating before fixing the ILIM resistor. `CAMERA_12V_EN` must default low during MCU reset. If the fault output is connected to firmware, record its active polarity and response in the camera-driver design notes.

### 5.8 `CAMERA_5V_SW` - retain for the MS2106E capture subsystem

| Item | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Upstream rail | `+5V_SYS` |
| Selected part | AP22653W6-7 |
| Firmware control | `CAMERA_5V_EN` from RP2354 |
| Downstream load | MS2106E CVBS-to-USB capture subsystem |
| Normal running state | On when the camera subsystem is needed |
| Vehicle off | Off |
| Crank policy | Off early |
| Primary reason | Power-cycle recovery, idle-current reduction, and keeping a USB video device absent until intentional start |

This is a distinct decision from camera 12 V. The 12 V branch powers the external camera. The 5 V branch powers the on-board video capture electronics. Firmware should treat them as a coordinated subsystem rather than unrelated GPIOs.

Initial policy to validate:

1. Enable capture electronics and camera power in a documented order.
2. Wait for regulator settling and USB enumeration.
3. On `REVERSE_OK`, tell Android to show the camera once the UVC device is ready.
4. Disable both rails during controlled shutdown and crank shedding.

Measure time-to-first-frame before choosing whether the subsystem can remain off until reverse is requested. If the delay is unacceptable, power the capture electronics earlier and only switch the external camera rail on demand.

### 5.9 `CAMERA_3V3` / `CAMERA_3V3A` - do not add a redundant switch yet

The MS2106E circuit has 3.3 V and filtered analog-3.3 V domains. Add a `CAMERA_3V3_SW` only if the camera electronics can otherwise remain partially powered after `CAMERA_5V_SW` is off. If that 3.3 V rail is generated only after the switched camera 5 V rail, a separate switch is redundant and introduces another sequencing dependency.

The ferrite-bead filtered `CAMERA_3V3A` domain is analog-noise control, not a power-switching mechanism.

The MS2106E path is intentionally retained as the first-board reverse-camera fallback. A future native-CM5 camera route requires a separately qualified CVBS-to-MIPI CSI-2 decoder, its own confirmed rail and reset requirements, Linux V4L2 bring-up, and Android camera-provider work. See [MIPI CSI-2 Reverse Camera Integration Plan](mipi-csi-camera-integration.md).

### 5.10 `FAN_12V` and fan PWM - regulation and speed control, not a discretionary switch

| Item | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Supply | Dedicated 12 V fan buck (`FAN_12V`) |
| Runtime control | `FAN_PWM`; `FAN_TACH` is monitoring feedback |
| Normal running state | Available whenever the main system is on; duty based on temperature |
| Vehicle off | Off with the main system |
| Crank policy | Do not make it the first branch to shed |
| Primary reason | Cooling is a service function; PWM adjusts load more gracefully than repeated hard power cuts |

The fan's regulated supply is not presently a branch switch. It should start at a conservative safe duty during thermal uncertainty, then follow the temperature policy. A hard fan supply switch is only warranted later if measured off-state current, wiring protection, or a specific fault strategy demands it.

### 5.11 Amplifier `AMP_STBY_CTL` / `AMP_MUTE_CTL` - signal control before supply switching

| Item | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Controls | `AMP_STBY_CTL`, `AMP_MUTE_CTL` from RP2354 |
| Normal running state | Standby released and unmuted only after audio path is ready |
| Vehicle off | Standby/mute asserted before global shutdown |
| Crank policy | First audio action: mute; then standby if needed |
| Primary reason | Avoid pops and reduce high audio load without adding another high-current rail switch |

For the initial amplifier approach, use mute/standby rather than a separate amplifier supply switch. Add a high-side supply switch later only if measured quiescent current, fault containment, or the final amplifier datasheet gives a concrete reason. The amplifier itself remains a major load, so it has high crank-shed priority even if the mechanism is control pins rather than a new power branch.

### 5.12 `IMMO_COIL_LO` - actuator driver, not a power-rail switch

`IMMO_COIL_LO` is the low-side connection for the externally wired relay coil driven by the smart N-channel MOSFET. It is intentionally categorized as an actuator output rather than system power management. Its default state must remain off during reset and loss of firmware control. Keep any vehicle safety, installation, legal, and failure-mode analysis in a dedicated actuator document rather than mixing it into the main rail sequencing rules.

### 5.13 `MODEM_3V3_SW` - dedicated SIM7600NAHPCIE power domain

| Item | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Upstream rail | `+5V_SYS`, through a modem-dedicated 3.3 V supply path |
| Controlled rail | `MODEM_3V3_SW`, 3.3 V nominal |
| Firmware control | `MODEM_PWR_EN` from U1003 P07 |
| Fault reporting | `~{MODEM_3V3_FLT}` to U1003 P17 from the final modem 3.3 V switch/protection path |
| Downstream load | SIM7600NAHPCIE mini-PCIe card VCC pins |
| Normal running state | On only after the main system is stable and firmware requests cellular/GNSS service |
| Vehicle off | Off |
| Crank policy | Shed early unless an eventual measured policy proves the radio can remain powered without threatening `+5V_SYS` |
| Primary reason | Controlled radio recovery, reduced vehicle-off current, and isolation of a high, bursty load from the shared digital 3.3 V rail |

The SIM7600 Series PCIE guide specifies a 3.0-3.6 V input, 3.3 V nominal, with **2 A supply capability**. Therefore `MODEM_3V3_SW` must not be supplied from the existing general-purpose AP63200 3.3 V rail and must not use the AP22653W6-7 as its 2 A supply path. Select a dedicated modem-rated 3.3 V regulator and high-side/disconnect implementation after the card's actual burst-current behavior is measured. The regulator enable may serve as the branch control only if the final circuit has a defined off state, safe output discharge, and no USB/control-line back-power path; otherwise retain a separate high-side switch.

`~{MODEM_3V3_FLT}` is an active-low status input, not another enable. Its final source is the modem branch's open-drain fault output. Pull it up to the local 3.3 V logic rail at that source, route it to U1003 P17, and treat an assertion as a modem-domain fault: log it, stop assuming the card is present, and use the defined modem recovery sequence rather than blindly re-enabling the branch in a tight loop.

This mini-PCIe card **auto powers on when its 3.3 V supply appears**. There is no `PWRKEY` sequence in this design. `MODEM_PWR_EN` is the hard power/recovery control, not a momentary button command.

The associated USB data path is `MODEM_USB_D+` / `MODEM_USB_D-` from the card edge to a dedicated CM5 USB host port. Route it as 90 ohm differential USB 2.0, retain nearby test points, and do not connect external USB VBUS to the card: SIMCom documents the module USB VBUS connection as internal.

### 6.2 GPIO expander policy

U1003 (TCA9539A-Q1) owns slow, non-time-critical rail enables and corresponding fault inputs. Its direct RP2354 control pins are `~{GPIO_EXP_INT}` on GPIO17 and `~{GPIO_EXP_RESET}` on GPIO18. The interrupt is open drain and must retain its 3.3 V pull-up. Firmware shall clear an asserted interrupt by reading the expander input port, then identify and log the source fault.

| TCA9539A-Q1 port | Assigned signal |
| --- | --- |
| P00-P06 | `LCD_BIAS_EN`, `LCD_1V8_EN`, `TOUCH_3V3_EN`, `BACKLIGHT_EN`, `USB_VBUS_EN`, `CAMERA_5V_EN`, `CAMERA_12V_EN` |
| P07 | `MODEM_PWR_EN`; active-high enable for `MODEM_3V3_SW` |
| P10 | `MODEM_PERST_ASSERT`; active-high command to an external N-MOS that pulls the card-edge `~{MODEM_PERST}` line low |
| P11 | `STATUS_LED` |
| P12-P16 | `~{LCD_BIAS_FLT}`, `~{TOUCH_3V3_FLT}`, `~{USB_VBUS_FLT}`, `~{CAMERA_5V_FLT}`, `~{CAMERA_12V_FLT}` |
| P17 | `~{MODEM_3V3_FLT}`; active-low fault input from the dedicated modem 3.3 V branch |

During initialization, hold the expander in reset, configure all branch-enable latches low, release reset, then explicitly write the safe output image before allowing normal sequencing. Time-critical paths remain direct RP2354 GPIOs: CAN SPI/interrupt/reset, I2S, amplifier control, fan control, and the system-hold path.

The following controls belong to RP2354 firmware, directly or through U1003. At reset they must resolve to the documented safe state through hardware defaults, not just firmware timing.

| Signal | Default | Firmware authority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `SYS_HOLD` | Low | Hold the global 5 V system rail only during controlled shutdown |
| `CM5_PWR_BUT_ASSERT` | Low | GPIO8; momentary open-drain CM5 power-button fallback, never a direct `SYS_HOLD` control |
| `RP2354_TO_CM5_ATTN_N` | Released high | GPIO32 open-drain doorbell; prompt the CM5 to read the USB management channel |
| `LCD_1V8_EN` | Low | U1003 P01; optional panel logic power-cycle/recovery |
| `LCD_BIAS_EN` | Low | U1003 P00; TPS65150 input/bias-domain control |
| `TOUCH_3V3_EN` | Low | U1003 P02; touch recovery and isolation |
| `BACKLIGHT_EN` | Low | U1003 P03; brightness/driver enable and first-level display load shed |
| `USB_VBUS_EN` | Low | U1003 P04; external USB VBUS power policy and fault recovery |
| `CAMERA_12V_EN` | Low | U1003 P06; external camera supply |
| `CAMERA_5V_EN` | Low | U1003 P05; MS2106E capture-subsystem supply |
| `MODEM_PWR_EN` | Low | U1003 P07; enable the dedicated 3.3 V modem branch only after the core system is valid |
| `MODEM_PERST_ASSERT` | Low | U1003 P10; command an open-drain reset assertion, never drive the raw card-edge reset line high |
| `~{MODEM_3V3_FLT}` | Deasserted high | U1003 P17 input; log and recover a modem 3.3 V branch fault under firmware policy |
| `STATUS_LED` | Off | U1003 P11; low-priority diagnostic indication |
| `AMP_STBY_CTL` | Assert safe state | Amplifier enable sequence and load shed |
| `AMP_MUTE_CTL` | Assert mute | Pop-free transitions and immediate crank response |
| `FAN_PWM` | Defined safe duty or off per fan specification | Thermal policy |
| `IMMO_RELAY_EN` | Low | Actuator output; never assert by default |

`ACC_OK`, `ILLUM_OK`, `REVERSE_OK`, `FAN_TACH`, `~{GPIO_EXP_INT}`, and the expander fault inputs are inputs to RP2354, not commanded outputs. They inform policy; they do not by themselves energize a high-load branch without firmware sequencing.

### 6.3 SIM7600NAHPCIE interface and control contract

| Interface or control | Board-level implementation | Rev-A ownership / policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Card VCC | `MODEM_3V3_SW` must feed every specified mini-PCIe VCC contact from a dedicated 3.3 V, 2 A-capable path | U1003 P07 via `MODEM_PWR_EN`; default off |
| Modem power fault | `~{MODEM_3V3_FLT}` from the dedicated branch's open-drain fault output, with a local 3.3 V pull-up | U1003 P17 input; record the fault and run the bounded modem recovery policy |
| USB 2.0 | `MODEM_USB_D+` / `MODEM_USB_D-` directly to a dedicated CM5 host port | CM5/Android owns enumeration, AT transport, data interface, and GNSS/NMEA access; RP2354 is not in the USB data path |
| `~{MODEM_PERST}` | Card pin 22; local 100 nF capacitor and ESD protection per SIMCom reference | U1003 P10 drives an N-MOS gate through a resistor; the N-MOS only pulls reset low. Provide a 100 kOhm gate pulldown so reset remains released while the expander is held in reset. Assert low for 100-500 ms only when an intentional modem reset is required. |
| `~{MODEM_W_DISABLE}` | Card pin 20; RF flight-mode control | Leave unconnected for Rev A and use the modem's AT functionality mode initially. A future hardware flight-mode control must be level-aware; do not attach a 3.3 V GPIO directly merely because the pin is available. |
| `~{MODEM_WAKE}` | Card pin 1; optional host wake indication | Leave unconnected for Rev A. If later needed for sleep/resume, add a documented level-translation and pull-up circuit before assigning another expander port or a direct RP2354 input. |
| `MODEM_LED_WWAN` | Card pin 42; optional network-status LED output | Leave unconnected for Rev A unless a dedicated cellular status LED is wanted. It is not required for modem control. |
| UART, I2C, PCM | Optional card-edge interfaces | Leave unconnected for Rev A. The module's UART and I2C are 1.8 V-domain interfaces; USB is the intended primary host transport. |

The controller-facing command names intentionally differ from the raw active-low card nets. `MODEM_PERST_ASSERT` is active high at the expander output because it turns on an N-MOS; the N-MOS then asserts the physical `~{MODEM_PERST}` line by pulling it low. This prevents an expander output from ever driving the module's internally pulled-up 1.8 V reset input high.

Recommended first-board sequence:

1. Set `MODEM_PWR_EN` low and ensure the reset-driver gate is low.
2. Bring `+5V_SYS` and its required CM5 USB-host resources up normally.
3. Enable `MODEM_PWR_EN`; the card auto starts when `MODEM_3V3_SW` becomes valid.
4. Wait for USB enumeration and use the CM5 modem software stack to establish LTE/GNSS operation.
5. For recovery, request a graceful modem shutdown through the USB/AT interface when possible. If needed, assert `MODEM_PERST_ASSERT` for the documented interval; use `MODEM_PWR_EN` only for a deliberate hard recovery after sufficient discharge time.

## 7. Fault and Back-Power Rules

Every switched branch must be checked for all three off-state problems:

1. **Signal back-powering:** An I2C, reset, interrupt, USB, or analog signal may feed an unpowered IC through its protection diode.
2. **Reverse current through the switch:** Select a part with the needed reverse blocking or prove it is not required.
3. **Partially powered subsystems:** Do not leave one rail applied to a multi-rail IC while another required rail is absent unless its datasheet explicitly allows that state.

The LCD translator solution is an example of a deliberate answer: VCCA remains on, VCCB is switched, both panel-side control lines are pulled low, and the translator is chosen for powered-off isolation. The camera is an example where this analysis is still required across `CAMERA_5V_SW`, `CAMERA_3V3`, and USB D+/D-.

## 8. Bring-Up Measurements Required Before Layout Freeze

1. Capture BATT+, protected 12 V, `+5V_SYS`, and CM5 5 V at startup and during a real crank event.
2. Measure steady, startup, and fault current for backlight, camera 12 V, camera 5 V, USB VBUS, LCD bias, touch, and amplifier standby/mute states.
3. Verify LCD sequencing: 1.8 V, bias rails, reset, standby, DSI activity, and panel response.
4. Verify that disabling each branch does not back-power it through its data/control lines.
5. Measure reverse-camera time from `REVERSE_OK` to first usable UVC frame.
6. Validate USB VBUS short-circuit and reconnect behavior with the final current limit.
7. Confirm that the fan remains controllable and that thermal management survives a low-voltage event.
8. Define a firmware timeout for every operation that waits for CM5 shutdown, USB enumeration, display initialization, or camera startup.
9. Validate `MODEM_3V3_SW` at minimum and maximum vehicle voltage, including modem transmit bursts, USB enumeration, GNSS operation, and a real crank event. Confirm the dedicated regulator, switch, connector, and copper can sustain the specified 2 A capability without compromising `+5V_SYS`.
10. Verify that a modem power-off does not back-power the card through USB D+/D-, `~{MODEM_PERST}`, or any optional card-edge interface.

## 10. Short Version

- **Global control** (`ACC_OK`, `SYS_HOLD`, `5V_SYS_GOOD`) decides whether the head unit is awake.
- **USB VBUS and camera 12 V** are mandatory branch switches because they reach the outside world.
- **Backlight and amplifier controls** are the first things to shed during crank.
- **LCD bias** is independently switchable because it is a meaningful analog power domain and enables a clean panel restart.
- **LCD 1.8 V and touch 3.3 V** are retained recovery/sequence switches, not primary crank switches. They give the first board deliberate post-crank and failed-initialization recovery paths.
- **Do not switch the level-shifter A-side 3.3 V**; it is controller-domain logic, and the selected translator handles the switched 1.8 V B-side correctly.
- **Camera 3.3 V gets its own switch only if it would otherwise keep the camera subsystem partially powered.**
- **The SIM7600NAHPCIE is a dedicated, switched 3.3 V radio domain:** it auto starts with power, needs a 2 A-capable supply path, and uses USB directly to the CM5. Reset is an open-drain active-low card signal, not a 3.3 V push-pull GPIO.
